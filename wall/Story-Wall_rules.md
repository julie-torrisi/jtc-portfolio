## Scale
7. Nothing scales. Components always stay at their original size: tiles grow to fit them, never the reverse (11/9). Clips may be cropped (never scaled) to keep equal padding. The page never zooms to fit the window.
7a. Videos stay as small as their tile allows so they read sharp. Trim any clip whose first frames show stray content.

## Grid and look
8. The whole wall drifts right to left as one sheet at 0.06 screen heights a second: 64.8 px/s at 1080 (checked against Story Factory at about 65 px/s and Rovo at about 140 px/s at 2284 tall).
9. Colors measured from Eduardo's video: wall #1F1E21, tiles #24232A, a soft shadow. Components sit inside the grey tile as their own card.
9a. No hairlines anywhere: tiles have no borders, never scale, and move on whole device pixels.
9b. One shared grid: 9 rows, 92 px units, 20 px gaps (v12, 11/9: more rows so more components show; replaces 7 × 128). Every tile is a whole multiple of the unit in both directions, with an equal gap on every side of every tile (J1). Inside a tile, the component sits with the same padding on all four sides (at least 24 px); the tile size snaps up to the next unit. How: the component's own card fills the tile with 24 px on every side and the component centers on it; a clip that can't grow sets its own equal pad (brand refresh tiles: 12 at 180 px; keyboard: 68; b1: 26; b2: 44). Small items sit in square one-unit tiles. Empty tiles are one unit each.
9c. The first frame is already full. Empty tiles in the tile color fill the grid between content tiles.
9d. The loop is wider than the screen, so a column never meets its own copy on screen.

## A tile entering, in order
10. Tiles ride the wall in: each tile already sits in its grid slot off the right edge and reaches the screen because the wall moves (J1). Nothing populates until the tile is fully in frame. Then:
   1. An empty shell at full size.
   2. A glow outline, once (J2, J3): a thin gradient traces the edge, lit on two or three sides with gaps, like one light source. Fade in about 150 ms, hold about 450 ms, fade out about 650 ms, drifting about 40° around the tile. Never loops. Colors: kit blue → lilac → mist. Amber (--ck-glow-warm) is a token used rarely: about one glow in nine gets the amber stop (11/9; see Gaps).
   3. The component builds in, each piece with its own entrance (J4):
      - Header first, then rows wipe in about 160 ms apart (thread rows and chat bubbles 160–200 ms).
      - App icons pop in, scaling up (J7); several in view enter about 250 ms apart.
      - Inputs type out letter by letter.
      - A logo traces itself as a stroke, then resolves to the filled mark.
      - Chart components (Blueprint bar and progress charts, J8): bars fill left to right and a donut sweeps clockwise from 12 o'clock; their numbers count up with the bar or sweep, in the number's own format. Count-up variant "tick": a +1 change rolls the digits up (old value out the top, new in from below, 300 ms, enter easing), used on the bling count (11/9). Numbers count (count-up-rule.md, rules 2–3; the final value is the arbitrary chart number from 6e, not a ledger number). Use /count-up wherever a number sits on a chart. On exported PNGs: an empty-bar copy sits under the export, the real bars reveal on top, a live number counts over the digits and lifts at the end so the last frame is the exported pixels.
   4. Then it holds still or keeps one slow loop (rule 13).

## Motion
12. Enter on out.bold, leave on in.practical, move on inout.bold. Spring only for people (cursors), reactions and the hanging card. Trays 300 ms ease-out. Count-ups ease-out quart (proposed, count-up-rule.md).
12a. Calm motion: at most three tiles build at the same time (v14, 11/10); the rest wait their turn in arrival order. Builds are slow (count-ups 1.6 s, rows 300 ms apart). Small static tiles only pop and never hold a turn. Charts always animate in, even on the first frame (v13, 11/10).
12b. Looping tiles (videos, the bling string) never touch another looping tile, not even at a corner or across the loop seam; the page checks this (window.__loops must be empty) (v14, 11/10). Brand-video clips dropped to make this fit: b1, b2, burst heart, rainbow, fire, like; the fa tile.
12c. Small static tiles (apps, stickers, Slide Reactions) each appear twice in the loop, about half a loop apart, never twice on screen (window.__smallNear must be empty) (v14).
13. Micro moments stay short: one looping element per tile, under 6 s a loop (the voice hold is about 4.5 s), so the eye can wander (J6, J9). Examples: the voice waveform, a drifting dot grid, a wordmark marquee, a spinner.
13a. Some cards stay still after they build.
13b. Small single-icon tiles always hold still once built (J10). That stillness makes the moving tiles readable.
13c. Hanging card (J5): the card swings once on entry as if hanging from a nail just above it; the nail, hook or string is never shown (11/10). Spring, pivot top center, about ±3°, settling in about 1.6 s (unchecked at 8 fps).
14. Figma cursors show first names only: Julie, Adam, Charlie, Eduardo, Jeremy, Josh. Julie's cursor is never purple or pink (it is Figma blue #1ABCFE) (v14, 11/10). They use the Comments-Wall cursor (Cursor · Name) at 1.5×: its arrow, a 57 px pill with 18 px radius, Inter Semi Bold 30 px; colors Julie #1ABCFE, Adam and Jeremy #A259FF, Charlie #FF7262, Eduardo #0ACF83, Josh #F5B400 with dark text (v13, 11/10). ONE at a time, never two on screen; a person may come back for another control. Each action happens once per tile entry (no double taps). The arrow's tip is the hit point and lands on the centre of the control itself. Cursors move slowly enough to read: about 1.8 s in, 1.4 s hold, 1.4 s out (11/9). Actions: Julie flips the switch, Adam taps the mic to record, Charlie taps a pill tab, Eduardo picks a reaction, Jeremy taps View, Josh taps a comment's reaction.

Every press shows Blueprint's Pressed State (Actions page): Non Media Pressed on Dark (white 30%) over the control on dark surfaces, Non Media Pressed (#46484B 15%) on light ones (the mic), buttons and the switch also scale to .97 for 100 ms (FDSButton). Applies to the switch, pill tab, comment reaction, mic, View and the UFI Like (11/9). Jeremy taps View once.

Large pointers with a colored name pill (22 px semibold label, 34 px pointer). Each enters from the nearest edge, presses one real control (switch, pill tab, mic, pinned message button, a reaction on a comment; the component steps to its next variant, or a tile builds in), holds a beat and leaves the way it came. A cursor never crosses the whole screen. The next starts only after the last is gone.
